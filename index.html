🪑 On the bench — tap to add back

Once someone passes the target, everybody else gets one last turn to beat them — and if somebody takes the lead, the lap starts over against them. Tap a player's icon to change it, or the arrows to change the turn order.

How scoring works here

Tap a button for each scoring combo you rolled — they stack up in your pot. Bank it to keep the points, or hit FARKLE when you roll nothing and lose the pot. Farkle rules vary by kitchen table, so you can name your own house rules and they stick around as buttons.

🎲 Scoring cheat sheet

The singles

  • Each 1 = 100
  • Each 5 = 50

Three of a kind

  • Three 1s = 1000
  • Three 2s = 200 · 3s = 300 · 4s = 400 · 5s = 500 · 6s = 600

More of a kind

  • Four of a kind = 1000
  • Five of a kind = 2000
  • Six of a kind = 3000

Special rolls

  • Straight 1-2-3-4-5-6 = 1500
  • Three pairs = 1500
  • Two triplets = 2500
  • Four of a kind + a pair = 1500

The turn

  • Roll six dice, set aside at least one scoring die, then re-roll the rest or bank.
  • Score with all six dice and you get "hot dice" — roll all six again and keep building.
  • Roll no scoring dice at all and it's a Farkle — the whole pot is gone.
  • With the 🚪 rule on, your first bank of the game has to reach the opening amount — set it to whatever your table plays on the setup screen.

🏁 Finishing

  • First past the target starts the last lap. Everybody else gets one turn to beat them, and each column shows exactly what that player needs.
  • Pass the leader and the lap resets around you — everyone gets a shot at your new number.
  • Go a full lap with nobody passing the leader and the leader wins.

🎲 Virtual dice

  • Shake the cup to spill six onto the felt. Six plain white dice — they tell you nothing about which of them are worth anything, so read your own roll.
  • Tap the ones you're keeping and they wear a gold ring — the bar underneath adds them up as you go, and it won't let you set aside a die that doesn't score.
  • Stuck? With nothing picked up, the bar names the biggest thing on the table and what it's worth. Tap the bar to take it in one go.
  • Set & roll banks those dice into your pot and throws the rest. Set all six and you get hot dice with a fresh six.
  • Roll nothing at all and the app calls the farkle for you.

🎠 Let it ride (roll with greed)

  • When someone banks and sits down, they leave their un-rolled dice on the table.
  • The next player can let it ride: pick up that banked total as their own starting pot and keep rolling with whatever dice are sitting there.
  • You have to score at least once on those dice before you can bank — no picking up a pot and walking off with it. Do it and the whole thing is yours. Farkle and you lose the lot — but the player who banked keeps their points either way.
  • The offer shows you both sides: the pot with the dice that come with it, against a fresh six with nothing in it. Not feeling lucky? Take the FRESH SIX ❄️.
  • If the get-on-the-board rule is on, nobody can ride until every player has banked their way onto the board.

🤖 Rollerbots

  • Tap the 🤖 beside a name on the setup screen and the app plays that seat. It rolls on the felt whether or not anyone else is, since it can't pick up the dice on your table.
  • 🐢 Careful — picks its dice well, then quits while it's ahead. Stops on three.
  • 🤖 Steady — plays it straight: it rolls whenever rolling is worth more than banking.
  • 😈 Gambler — leaves dice on the table for the chance of a bigger throw, rolls on two, and now and then goes for it on one. It farkles nearly twice as often as the others.
  • 🤪 Screwball — draws a new temperament every turn and tells you what it is. Some turns it's more cautious than Careful, some turns it's barely sane, and one turn in six it forgets strategy and just grabs whatever scores.
  • All of them work out that a 1 with three 2s is worth more as just the 1 — 100 points and five dice to throw beats 300 points and two.
  • Three dice score seven throws in ten, so nobody hands over three or more — they stand up and throw them, whatever the pot, target crossed or not. What you get offered comes with one or two dice behind it, not a handful.
  • Except the 🐢, which will sit on three if the pot is under 1,200 — and even then only half the time. It quits while it's ahead; that's the whole of it.
  • The 😈 dithers on the small ones: under 500 with a die or two left it tosses a coin to throw again, and again on what that leaves. Over 500 it banks like everyone else.
  • Nobody can ride until everyone is on the board, so while anybody is still short of it there's nothing to protect: the 🐢 and 🤖 bank the moment they're over the opening amount and leave whatever's lying there, unless they've just swept all six. The 😈 holds on for a bigger handful, but banks on three or fewer.
  • Undo during a robot's turn parks it. Tap the bar under the felt to set it going again.
